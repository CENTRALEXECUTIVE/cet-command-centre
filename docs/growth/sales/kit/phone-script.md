# 60-second follow-up call script

**Who it's for:** a PA or EA, an office manager, or a travel or facilities manager
who has had at least email 1 from us. **The purpose isn't to sell on the phone.**
The aim is to get one of three outcomes: a 10-minute call in the diary, permission
to send the rate card, or the name of the right person.

**Before you dial (30 seconds)**
- Check Outreach: what you sent, when, and the angle (visitors, finance, events).
- Use the published business number. Never ring a personal mobile you found online.
- Make sure your number shows when you call. Don't withhold it.
- [CONFIRM: Corporate TPS screening process. Until that's agreed, only call
  prospects who replied, gave a number, or asked to be called.]
- Have open: `objection-handling.md`, and Outreach so you can log the call.
- Not while you're driving. Not while a passenger is in the car.

Speak slowly and plainly. Say your name and company in the first sentence.

---

## The call (about 60 seconds)

**1. Opening (10 seconds)**
> "Hello [Name], it's Maj from Central Executive Transfers in Sheffield. Have you got
> 60 seconds, or have I caught you at a bad time?"

If it's a bad time: *"No problem. When's better, later today or tomorrow morning?"*
Book the time, log it, and end the call.

**2. Reason for the call (10 seconds)**
> "I emailed you last week about visitor and airport travel. I'm one of the two
> directors, and I wanted to put a voice to the email rather than send another one."

**3. One question (15 seconds).** Ask it, then stop talking.
> "Out of interest, when you have a visitor flying into Manchester or East Midlands,
> or someone coming in by train, how do you usually get them here?"

Other versions you can use:
- Law, accountancy or professional firms: *"When partners go to London or the airport, who books the car?"*
- HR or recruitment: *"When you bring a candidate in for interview, do you arrange their travel?"*
- Events: *"For your bigger events, do you have a local car firm on call?"*

**4. One relevant point (15 seconds).** Pick the one that fits their answer.
- **They use a taxi firm or an app:** *"That's fine. The difference with us is a fixed
  price agreed up front, and one VAT invoice at the end of the month instead of receipts."*
- **The PA worries about visitors:** *"Before each pickup you get the driver's name,
  the vehicle and a live tracking link, and we track the flight, so you're not chasing anyone."*
- **They want to deal with someone local:** *"We're Sheffield-based and owner-run. A
  director answers the phone, and the directors drive the executive jobs."*

**5. The ask (10 seconds)**
> "Would it be useful if I sent our corporate rate card for your two or three most
> common routes? If you tell me which ones, I'll make it specific."

Or: *"Could we put 10 minutes in the diary next week, so I can understand how you book?"*

**If they say yes, capture these before you hang up:**
- Their usual routes (for example, Sheffield to Manchester Airport, or the station to site)
- Roughly how many journeys a month, and what vehicle (saloon or a group)
- Who books, and who approves new suppliers (procurement form?)
- Invoicing needs: PO or cost-centre numbers, payment terms, invoice email address
- The best email address for the rate card

Close with: *"Thank you. I'll send that today."* Then do it. If the rate card
isn't approved yet, send `why-cet-for-business-v2.md` and give a specific quote
for their routes.

**If they say no:**
> "That's fine, thank you for being straight with me. Shall I leave you off our list
> altogether, or is it all right to check in next year?"

Log the answer. **"Leave me off" means Do not contact.**

## Gatekeeper handling (reception or switchboard)

Be polite and honest, and don't pretend they're expecting you.

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "What's it regarding?" | "I'm Maj, a director at Central Executive Transfers, a car firm in Sheffield. I emailed [Name] about travel for visitors and wanted to follow up. Is [he/she] free for a minute?" |
| "Is [he/she] expecting your call?" | "No, it's a follow-up to an email I sent. If now's not good, when's the best time to catch [him/her]?" |
| "We don't take sales calls." | "Understood. Who's the right person to email about local transport or supplier set-up? I'll write instead." |
| "Just send an email." | "Happy to. Is [Name]'s address the best one, or does someone else look after travel bookings?" Then log and stop. Don't argue. |
| "[Name] doesn't deal with that." | "Thank you, that's helpful. Who does book cars for visitors or the leadership team?" Note the role, not just the name. |
| "They're in a meeting / not in today." | "No problem. Is there a time that usually works, or shall I try [day]?" Don't leave a message with reception unless they offer. |
| "We already use another firm." | "That's fine, most companies have someone. Could I send [Name] our rate card as a back-up for when they can't cover?" |

**Two tries with the gatekeeper at most per prospect.** After that, email only.

## Voicemail (about 20 seconds, leave it once)

> "Hello [Name], it's Maj from Central Executive Transfers in Sheffield, on 0114 399 8242.
> I emailed you last week about visitor and airport travel: fixed prices and one
> monthly VAT invoice. No need to call back. I'll send you a short email so you have
> my details. Again, that's Maj, 0114 399 8242. Thanks."

Then send a short email in the same thread:

> Hello [Name],
>
> I've just left you a short voicemail, so here are my details in writing. If it's
> useful, I can send our corporate rate card for your usual routes. Just reply with
> the two or three you use most.
>
> [Signature]
> [Opt-out line]

The call, the voicemail and this short email all sit alongside the email sequence.
They don't count as one of the three touches and they don't move the day-10 or
day-18 dates. One call and one voicemail per prospect per sequence, no more.

---

## After every call (30 seconds)

Add a note in Outreach: who you spoke to (role first), what they said, and the
next action with a date. For example: *"Call: spoke to PA (Sarah). Uses a local
taxi account, unhappy with invoicing. Send rate card for MAN and LBA. Due Thu."*
