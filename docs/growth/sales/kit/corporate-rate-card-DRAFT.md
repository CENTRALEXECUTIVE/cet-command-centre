## Part 3: Optional account discount (directors to decide; internal only)

**Read this first.** Driver pay defaults to **90% of the fare**, and the company
keeps 10%. A straight 10% discount on a job covered by a partner driver wipes out
the company's share completely, unless driver pay is worked out on the
pre-discount fare. On jobs the directors drive, a discount comes straight out of
your own pocket. Whatever you choose, decide whether driver pay is calculated on
the discounted fare or the full fare.

| Option | What it is | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. No discount** | Public matrix prices; the value is the invoicing, the fixed price and the service. | Protects the margin. Simple. | Some procurement teams expect "a corporate rate". |
| **B. Flat account rate** | [CONFIRM: x]% off the matrix for every account client. | Easy to explain. | Costs the margin on every job, including tiny accounts. |
| **C. Included extras instead of money off** | Meet and greet included, plus [CONFIRM] extra free waiting at airports, child seats waived, and prices locked for 12 months. | Feels generous and costs little. Price lock is a strong point for finance teams. | Less eye-catching than a percentage. |
| **D. Volume rebate** | [CONFIRM: y]% credit on the next invoice once monthly spend passes £[CONFIRM]. | Only rewards real volume. | Slightly more admin each month. |

**Suggestion for discussion:** start with **C** for every account, add **D** for
accounts that genuinely reach the threshold, and keep **B** in reserve for a formal
tender where a percentage is required. Don't offer any discount on a first call.
Take it back to the directors.
