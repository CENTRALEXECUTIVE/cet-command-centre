# CET B2B email style guide

How Central Executive Transfers writes first-touch emails to the people who arrange
travel: EAs and PAs, office managers, events teams, case managers, and partner or
operations managers at other chauffeur firms. Every prospect CSV in
`docs/growth/sales/` follows this guide. Facts come only from
`docs/growth/BUSINESS_FACTS.md`.

## Who we are, in one breath

Central Executive Transfers is a **chauffeur company**, not an airport-taxi firm.
Experienced, professional chauffeurs for corporate and executive travel of every
kind: every UK airport, rail stations, meetings and roadshows across the UK, site
visits, client and VIP hosting, events and hospitality, hourly and as-directed hire,
day hire with waiting, delegates and small groups (V-Class, minibus), and London
runs. Airports are one part of it, not the headline.

## Voice rules

1. **Write as "we", signed "Abdi & Maj".** Use "We run Central Executive Transfers…",
   "We're the directors of…", or "Central Executive Transfers is a Sheffield chauffeur
   company, run by the two of us." Never mix in "I". Never open with "We are an
   owner-run…".
2. **About them, not us.** The first line is about the reader's world: their visitors,
   their clients, their guests, their site. Keep it light and natural, the sort of
   thing you would say on the phone. One sentence.
3. **No scraped openers.** Banned: "We saw that…", "We read in [publication]…",
   "I noticed…", "Congratulations on…", "Well done on…". Never recite press
   releases, headcounts, £ figures, contract values or award names back at them.
   If the only hook is an unverified news fact, write a solid generic opener instead.
4. **No feature dumps.** Do not list masked numbers, live tracking links, PO
   references, 30-day terms, seat counts or licence numbers in the body. Choose the
   one or two things that matter to this reader (for a PA: dependable, one account,
   one monthly VAT invoice; for a case manager: calm, vetted, door to door, case
   reference; for a partner: unbranded, your client stays yours, 24/7). Everything
   else waits for the reply.
5. **Credentials stay out of the body.** Operator licence, company number and
   insurance belong in an attachment or a reply. The exceptions are supplier
   registration enquiries to public bodies and portal applications, where licensing
   is the point.
6. **One ask, low friction.** "Would it be useful if we sent over our account
   details?" / "Is there someone who looks after travel we should speak to?" /
   "Would you be open to a short conversation about how that could work?" Never two
   asks. Never "book a demo", never a calendar link.
7. **60 to 110 words** in the body, excluding the signature. Three short paragraphs:
   their world, who we are and what we would do for them, the ask.
8. **Subject lines** are short, plain and specific to them: "Visitor travel for
   Templeborough", "A chauffeur partner for your guests", "Supplier registration
   enquiry". No clickbait, no "Re:", no "Quick question", no exclamation marks.
9. **Only true facts.** No prices, no client names (say "South Yorkshire businesses"
   at most), no "No.1", no "leading", no "premier", no hype. British English. No
   emojis. No exclamation marks.
10. **Medical and care rows:** never claim wheelchair-accessible vehicles, escorts,
    passenger assistants or medical training. Where it matters (councils, care homes,
    NHS transport), say plainly that we don't run wheelchair-accessible vehicles.
11. **Format in the CSVs:** body starts `Hello,\n\n`, no `[Name]` placeholders, no
    `[CONFIRM …]` and no "verify" notes, literal `\n` for line breaks, then the
    standard signature and opt-out line below.

## The five segment templates

Words in `{braces}` get personalised for each row from `why_they_need_us`, `angle`,
`segment` and `town`. Everything else can stay as written.

### 1. Corporate, manufacturing and professional services

**Subject:** Visitor and executive travel for {site or town}

```
Hello,

{One light line about their world, e.g. "With customers and colleagues from overseas
visiting Templeborough, we imagine travel is arranged most weeks."}

We run Central Executive Transfers, a Sheffield chauffeur company looking after
executives, visitors and clients for businesses across South Yorkshire. That covers
airport and station collections, meetings and site visits anywhere in the UK, and day
hire with waiting when the diary is full. It all runs on one account, with a single
monthly VAT invoice.

Would it be useful if we sent over our account details?
```

Variants: law and accountancy swap in "Our chauffeurs are experienced and discreet,
which matters when the passenger is a client" and "a matter reference for each
journey". Recruiters swap in candidate travel ("a candidate's journey to a final
interview is part of the impression your client makes"). Large firms that may book
through a national provider get "we're happy to act as the local supplier".

### 2. Rehabilitation, medical and case management

**Subject:** Client journeys across South Yorkshire

```
Hello,

{One light line, e.g. "Your case managers arrange a lot of journeys for clients, to
therapy, clinics and expert appointments."}

We run Central Executive Transfers, a Sheffield chauffeur company. Our chauffeurs are
experienced, council-licensed and enhanced-DBS checked, and they understand that some
passengers need a calm, unhurried journey. We collect door to door, can wait and bring
the client home, and invoice monthly with your case or claim reference on every
journey.

Is there someone who arranges client travel we should speak to?
```

Hospitals and clinics: "Patients can pay when they book, or the hospital can be
invoiced monthly with a reference per patient." Care settings: add "We don't run
wheelchair-accessible vehicles" and keep to residents who can walk to the car,
visiting families and staff.

### 3. Venues, hotels and events

**Subject:** A chauffeur partner for your guests

```
Hello,

{One light line, e.g. "With business guests, conferences and weddings, your front desk
must be asked for a reliable car most days."}

We run Central Executive Transfers, a chauffeur company based in Sheffield. We'd like
to be a partner your team can recommend with confidence: transfers to any UK airport or
station, collections for speakers and VIPs, evening runs home after events, and V-Class
or minibus for small groups. Prices are agreed in advance and organisers can be
invoiced directly.

Would you be open to a short conversation about how that could work?
```

Wedding venues: "a Rolls-Royce Ghost on request" for the couple. Never mention
commission or referral fees in a first email.

### 4. Public bodies, councils and the NHS

**Subject:** Supplier registration enquiry

```
Hello,

We run Central Executive Transfers, a licensed private hire operator based in
Sheffield, and we'd like to understand how to register as a transport supplier to
{organisation}.

We provide experienced, enhanced-DBS-checked chauffeurs for {visiting guests, staff
travel and planned journeys} across South Yorkshire and the UK, with prices agreed in
advance and monthly invoicing from our VAT-registered company.

Could you point us to the right portal or the person who manages transport suppliers?
```

Passenger transport tenders (SEND, NEPTS): state plainly that our fleet is saloons,
estates and seven-seat people carriers, not wheelchair-accessible vehicles, and that we
don't supply escorts or clinical staff.

### 5. Trade partners, international networks and TMCs

**Subject:** A South Yorkshire partner for your clients

```
Hello,

{One light line, e.g. "When your clients' plans take them to Sheffield, South
Yorkshire or the East Midlands, we'd like to be the partner you call."}

We run Central Executive Transfers, a chauffeur company based in Sheffield. Our
chauffeurs are experienced and discreet, the service is unbranded so your client stays
yours, and we're available 24/7. Work in your area that comes our way would go to you.

Would a short call to see whether there's a fit be useful?
```

TMCs: drop the reciprocal line and ask "Is there someone who looks after ground
transport suppliers we should speak to?" Platforms without an API: say once, plainly,
that we run our own dispatch system.

## Good versus bad

### Example 1: corporate

**Bad**
> We saw the £19.3m investment in the Advanced Blade Casting Facility announced in
> March, aiming to double output by 2030. We're the two directors of Central Executive
> Transfers, a licensed Sheffield chauffeur firm (operator licence OP037). Your team
> would get prices fixed in advance, flight-tracked meet and greet, and driver details
> plus a live tracking link before every pickup…

Recites their press release, leads with a licence number, lists five features and
treats the whole business as airport pickups.

**Good**
> A site like the Advanced Blade Casting Facility tends to have a steady flow of
> engineers, auditors and colleagues travelling between Rotherham, Derby and the
> airports.
>
> We run Central Executive Transfers, a Sheffield chauffeur company looking after
> executives, visitors and clients for businesses across South Yorkshire…

### Example 2: rehab and case management

**Bad**
> We read in NR Times how NCM UK grew from one case manager and a rehab coordinator…
> Calls and texts go through a masked number. Each journey carries your client or claim
> reference on one monthly VAT invoice, on 30-day terms…

Sounds scraped, then turns into a spec sheet.

**Good**
> Your case managers arrange a lot of journeys for clients, to therapy, clinics, expert
> appointments and conferences with counsel, and we're close neighbours in South
> Yorkshire.
>
> We run Central Executive Transfers, a Sheffield chauffeur company. Our chauffeurs are
> experienced, council-licensed and enhanced-DBS checked, and they understand that some
> passengers need a calm, unhurried journey…

### Example 3: trade partner

**Bad**
> Your "Beyond London" piece shows Addison Lee following corporate clients well outside
> the capital… Your passenger sees an unbranded driver link with no CET branding, plus
> driver details, live tracking and masked numbers. Flights are tracked. You'd get one
> cover invoice per period from our VAT-registered Ltd…

Quotes their blog back at them and buries the one point a partner cares about.

**Good**
> When Addison Lee's corporate clients travel beyond London, some of those journeys
> will start or finish in Sheffield, South Yorkshire or the East Midlands.
>
> We run Central Executive Transfers, a chauffeur company based in Sheffield, and we'd
> like to be the affiliate you call for that work. Our chauffeurs are experienced and
> discreet, the service is unbranded so your client stays yours, and we're available
> 24/7…

## Portal and application rows

Rows whose subject begins `PORTAL APPLICATION` or `APPLICATION` are not cold emails.
The subject column is an action note for the director and stays as written. Where the
body is empty, leave it empty: the director fills in the portal form. Where a body
exists, it is a short paste-ready statement for the form's message field. That is the
one place company number and operator licence belong in the body. The director can drop
the opt-out line when pasting into a form.
