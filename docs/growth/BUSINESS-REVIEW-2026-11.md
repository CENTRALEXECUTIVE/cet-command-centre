## 5. What to improve

1. **Margin on external drivers.** 10% on an external driver's job is about £17. After
   payment fees, dispatch time, the phone line and insurance, that leaves very little. See
   option (c).
2. **Pricing structure.** Under a 90/10 split, a price rise mostly goes to the driver. A 5%
   rise on £174 is £8.70; the company keeps 87p. If the company needs money, the change has
   to be to its share or a small booking fee it keeps (option d).
3. **Cash discipline.** Prepaid money has been spent. Ring-fence it (2.7), pay in the agreed
   order (2.6), and write down what is owed to each director every week.
4. **Payment timing.** Keep card-at-booking for private customers. Account clients are on 30
   days: chase on day 31, and invoice the day after each job, not monthly, while cash is
   tight **[CONFIRM clients accept this]**.
5. **Customer concentration.** By count it is spread (the four biggest repeat customers made
   about 7% of bookings), but Manchester Airport is about 40% of trips and executive saloon
   80%. One quiet month at Manchester hurts.
6. **Marketing.** There is no paid channel now, and the free ones take months. Put the effort
   where the plan's own data says it pays: win-back, partners, accounts, Google Business
   Profile and reviews.
7. **Data gaps.** VAT owed, older prepaid jobs, external drivers' pay this week, how many
   jobs are cash, and trade rates for partners are all unknown. Each one affects the cash

## 6. Options with numbers

Winter figures use 30 trips a month and £174 a trip unless stated.

**(a) Stay no-ads.** Cost £0. Expected 29 to 33 trips a month (low 20 to 25). Company keeps
about £505 to £575 a month against £350 overheads. Upside: no cash risk. Downside: few new
customers (about 8 to 9 a month), so the repeat base slowly shrinks, and directors' driving
pay falls to about £2,800 to £4,100 a month combined.

**(b) Lean ads, £10 a day** (brand, Sheffield to Manchester exact, Heathrow). About £304 a
month. If it buys new customers at the old £63 each **[ASSUMPTION: a small budget may do
better or worse]**, that is about 5 trips (£870 of fares). Company keeps £87, so the company
loses about £217 a month. Counting the directors' driving pay on those trips, the pair of you
gain roughly £870 in fares for £304. Note: the growth plan's restart rule says exclude brand
(people searching your name may find you anyway). Only worth it if you have spare driving
hours and agree the cost comes out of your driving income, not the company.

**(c) Raise the company's share on external-driver jobs from 10% to 15% or 20%.** In winter,
external jobs are mostly V-Class/minibus, about 20% of trips **[ASSUMPTION]**: 6 trips, about
£1,044 a month. +5 points = about £52 a month; +10 points = about £104. At summer volumes
(external driver pay averaged £7,128 a month Jun to Sep, about £7,920 of fares) the same
change is worth about £400 to £790 a month. Downside: a good V-Class or minibus driver may
leave, and you would have to turn work away or pass it to other operators. Give a month's
notice, talk to each driver, and apply it to new bookings only **[CONFIRM what local operators
pay their drivers before choosing the level]**.

**(d) Small price rises.** A 5% fare rise adds about £8.70 a trip but only 87p to the company
(about £26 a month), and the rest to the driver. A flat £5 booking fee kept by the company
adds about £150 a month (a 30% lift on its cut). Downside: you have no data on how price-
sensitive your airport customers are, and Manchester runs are easy to compare online. Test on
new private bookings only, and keep account prices as agreed.

**(e) Partner and cover work.** The plan expects 2 to 5 jobs a month per partner once live,
first jobs in 2 to 4 weeks **[ASSUMPTION; trade rates not yet decided]**. At a trade fare of
£120 to £160 **[ASSUMPTION]**, the company keeps £12 to £16 a job, and the driver (often one of
you) about £108 to £144. Upside: fills quiet winter hours with no marketing cost. Downside:
lower fares, slow vetting, and partners expect fast replies at all hours.

**(f) Retention and win-back.** Free. Returning customers are about 20 trips a month in the
forecast. A personal Christmas/New Year message to past private customers and a referral ask
to the three account clients could add 3 to 6 trips in December **[ASSUMPTION]**, about £520
to £1,040 of fares. Downside: only time. Never auto-send; a director sends each one.

### Suggested combination (your decision)

1. **Now:** (f) win-back and (e) partners. Free, and the fastest way to fill your own hours.
2. **From 1 December, with notice:** (c) move external-driver jobs to 15% on new bookings,

3. **Keep ads off** until the week-of-9-Nov result. If that week and the next are both 5
